\section{Problem statement and objective}
Develop and compare four autoregressive (next-word) text-generation models ---
Simple RNN, Bidirectional RNN, LSTM, and GRU --- on a chosen textual corpus. Each model
predicts $w_{t+20}$ from context $[w_t,\dots,w_{t+19}]$. Performance is evaluated using
loss, perplexity, prediction accuracy, and training time, as well as qualitative coherence,
diversity, contextual consistency, and repetition of generated text, under three sampling
temperatures $T\in\{0.5,1.0,1.5\}$.

\section{Dataset description and source}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Dataset:} \textit{The War of the Worlds}, H. G. Wells (public domain).
\item \textbf{Source:} \url{https://www.gutenberg.org/files/36/36-0.txt} (Project Gutenberg eBook \#36).
\item Tokens after cleaning: $\sim$70k; vocabulary $\sim$3.1k (minimum frequency 2).
\item Split: 80/10/10 train/validation/test; context length 20 (many-to-one target avoids
  future-token leakage for the Bidirectional model).
\end{itemize}

\section{Models and experimental conditions}
All models share: embedding dim 128, hidden dim 256, 1 layer, dropout 0.2, Adam
($\mathrm{lr}=10^{-3}$), gradient clipping $1.0$, CrossEntropyLoss, seed 42.
The Bidirectional model splits the hidden size across directions.

\[
\mathrm{PPL} = \exp(\mathcal L), \qquad
P(w)\propto \exp(z_w/T).
\]

\section{Model comparison}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{LSTM} achieves the lowest validation/test perplexity and accuracy.
\item \textbf{GRU} is close (87.13 vs 86.72 PPL) while training $\sim$22\% faster and uses fewer parameters.
\item \textbf{BiRNN} showed only marginal benefit because predictions are causal (left context).
\item \textbf{Simple RNN} trains fastest but underfits longer dependencies.
\end{itemize}

\section{Sampling-temperature analysis}
Text was sampled from identical seeds at $T=0.5,1.0,1.5$ (outputs in
\texttt{results/generated\_samples.txt}).
\begin{itemize}
\item $T=0.5$: conservative and repetitive (high $<$unk$>$ repetition), most grammatically stable.
\item $T=1.0$: best balance of coherence and diversity; recommended default.
\item $T=1.5$: highest diversity but frequent drift, odd word choices and punctuation.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\textbf{LSTM} is the best-performing model on held-out perplexity and accuracy. \textbf{GRU}
is effectively tied and more efficient, so it is the recommended practical choice for
iterative autoregressive generation on this corpus. Simple RNN and Bidirectional RNN remain
viable but less accurate.
